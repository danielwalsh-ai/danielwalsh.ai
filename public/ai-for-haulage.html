AI for haulage and transport firms

Most transport bosses can't say which wagons made money last month. Not because they're daft, but because the numbers live in five places and two people's heads. I build systems that put them in one place, every morning, automatically. I work hands-on in haulage every week, so this page is written from the traffic office, not a textbook.

The numbers arrive too late to act on

By the time the accounts package tells you a contract wasn't worth the diesel, you've run it for another six weeks. Earnings sit in one spreadsheet, workshop costs in another, fuel in a portal, tachograph and MOT compliance somewhere else, and the person who understands it all is on holiday. Meanwhile the office headcount creeps up just to keep the paperwork moving.

That gap between what's happening in the yard and what you can see on paper is where the margin goes. Closing it is exactly the kind of job AI does well, because it's the same steps, every day, from the same sources.

What AI actually does inside a haulage firm

  • Wagon by wagon profit. Earnings against target, per truck, per day, landing in your inbox before the kettle's boiled. Faster than any accounts package can manage, because it doesn't wait for month end.
  • Fleet and workshop costs. Parts, labour and downtime split by wagon and wagon type, with running costs separated from capital spend, so keep-or-sell decisions make themselves.
  • VOR tracking. Which wagons are off the road, how long, why, and who the repeat offenders are, reported daily without anyone compiling anything.
  • Compliance paperwork. MOT dates, defect reports and maintenance records tracked and chased automatically instead of living on a whiteboard.
  • The daily admin. Load sheets read automatically, POD chasing, invoice queries answered from the data instead of from memory.

These aren't ideas. They're descriptions of systems I've already built, running every morning inside a haulage group whether anyone remembers them or not.
